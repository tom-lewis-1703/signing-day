# Replacement transfer market v1.5 — acceptance and stopping conditions

Owner: Astra. Revised October 22, 2026 for specification v1.5 at `docs/replacement-transfer-spec-v1.md`: full schools may make one contingent offer per room per round, the competition trigger counts real misses only (v1.3), a full-board school may release one 1–2★ non-Star/Elite recruit as part of an accepted transfer offer, HB rotation is 2 (v1.4), and the second HB weighs 0.50 with opportunity 70 (v1.5). Check that every released recruit was eligible, released only by an accepted offer, at most one per school, and saved as Top10 with zero offers and no board membership. Seed names keep their `sd-v1.2-` prefix. These are prospective checks, not passed results. No implementation experiment or save run was performed in this revision. The archived v1.1 documents are under `test_runs/replacement-transfer-design-history/v1.1-before-v1.2/`.

The purpose is to establish that the implementation follows the design and that its decisions form a credible, varied market on the available evidence. Existing baseline destinations/counts are not targets. A favorable league average cannot excuse contradictory choices. Claude performs implementation, tests, and routine debugging; Astra reviews central code and selected raw traces and owns acceptance. Tom performs any required in-game check.

Tom has chosen a standalone Signing Day tool. Assess this first offseason; weekly operation and persistent personalities across seasons are outside the current scope. Judge the release by plausible, varied individual decisions and enjoyable next-season teams. Creative simulated motives and rare understandable exceptions are part of the intended experience; universal placement, perfectly filled rosters, and equal talent distribution are not goals. Numerical variation alone does not establish interesting individuality. Generated motives must drive choices before they happen and must be labeled as simulation.

Tom recruits normally in the game and finishes his own recruiting before using the tool on a Signing Day save. He explicitly selects his user team, reviews the CPU market, and explicitly Saves. His roster and all existing commitments remain protected. Every other active transfer recruit enters the market, including CPU commitments and uncommitted players he pursued. The tool neither recruits automatically for him nor gives him retroactive recruiting opportunities; the game's preceding recruiting competition remains part of his experience. The existing user-protection and GUI checks below establish this boundary.

Revision 1.2 changes the model, random-key domain, and acceptance questions: concurrent offers across rooms, TE/FB support value, discounted succession value and explicit developmental promises, simulated lifestyle/culture preferences and school philosophy, a simpler default seed, direct roster comparisons, and early withdrawal proof. These are specified design choices, not demonstrated improvements. Implementation authorization remains separate and is carried manually by Tom to Claude. Do not execute these future checks merely because this document has been revised.

### C. Severe capacity and promise risks

Target these with explicit constructed states; random repetition is a poor substitute:

- A full school can replace a low-value veteran only on acceptance; it cannot cut its sole required K/P or reduce a pre-existing thin room below its frozen floor. A third allowed discretionary cut can occur; a fourth cannot. A keep lock can make the otherwise best cut unavailable.
- Baseline overage trims recompute marginal loss after each removal, from the roster after CPU transfer withdrawals. If protections prevent reaching 85, all that school's provisional veteran trims are discarded and the school is excluded. Include >85 protected HS/JUCO commitments. No HS/JUCO commitment is rescinded to solve this case. Native CPU transfers still enter the market even if their old school is excluded.
- An apparently free roster slot with a full actual commitment board cannot receive a transfer. A veteran cut does not free a board slot. Include a one-slot board, more than one attractive room, and simultaneous offers from other schools to expose reservation errors. Duplicate/aliased board ownership or aliased mutable prospect storage must fail preflight, not produce two signings into one slot.
- For an E=1 signer or one whose original O1>=35, three later additions may each look like a small loss relative to the preceding state but exceed 12 in total: the offending transaction must be blocked relative to the original snapshot. Future F protection applies to every E>=2 signer. Include a younger developmental reserve with original O1<35: its contract has no immediate O1 floor, so a later current-season upgrade may be legal if F remains within 12 and U stays above original outside. Include separate cases where each remaining condition fails. The threshold is evaluated at acceptance, never reclassified afterward. No tolerance ratcheting or retroactive conversion of a protected offer into a developmental one is permitted.
- The selected user school is absent from all offers/cuts. A former user-school player already teamless can move elsewhere. Missing/invalid selection fails before planning. A notAt lock removes only the intended pair. New signings cannot be subsequently cut or offered elsewhere.

- A full board initially occupied partly by CPU transfers gains exactly those slots during normalization; new signers at the same original school consume slots normally. Withdrawal does not consume the veteran-cut allowance. Active already-rostered incoming transfers or invalid former CPU signees block the plan explicitly; they cannot be omitted while retaining their commitments. These are acceptance obligations, not a request to repeat nearly identical cases for every position or seed. Use a compact, legible suite with expected values stated outside the implementation.
