Reading the save

lib/dynasty.mjs

The tool opens the save with the madden-franchise library and reads only the fields it uses, once (about 0.65 s). Then it builds lookups so nothing scans the whole player table twice.

Tables it reads

TableWhat it gives us
PlayerName, team, previous team, position, OVR, year, redshirt, dev trait, archetype, dealbreaker, home state, skill caps, every rating
TeamName, offense and defense scheme, prestige (0–10), Signing Day board, links to school grades and coach perks
RecruitRecruit class (high school, JUCO, transfer), recruit stage, link to his Top 10
Top 10 listsEach recruit's 10 schools and influence (high-school recruits and transfers use different tables; the tool follows each link)
Leaving playersEarly-draft and portal decisions
Transfer queueWho the game is processing into the portal
School gradesEach school's dealbreaker grades (A+ to F), Pro Potential by position group, Playing Style by archetype
Coach talent effectsEach coach's transfer-interest perk and dealbreaker-threshold perk, by position group

Saving safely

lib/safe-save.mjs

V1Backup, write, verify, replace

Opening never changes the file. Only the Save button writes, in four steps; if any step fails, the original is untouched.

  1. Copy the save to a TTT Backups folder next to it, with a timestamp, and check the copy byte for byte (SHA-1).
  2. Write the changed save to a temporary file.
  3. Reopen the temporary file and check that the stage and the player count are unchanged.
  4. Replace the original with the temporary file. Backups are never deleted automatically.

Draft lock

lib/draft-lock.mjs · step 2

L1Who gets locked

Runs on the Players Leaving save. Only players out of eligibility may leave early for the NFL.

  • Locked: every player with an early-draft record who is not out of eligibility and not on Kansas.
  • What it writes: each of his early-draft records gets LeaveStatus = Staying and PersuadeAttempts ≥ 1, the same values the game writes when you persuade a player.
  • Left alone: graduating players, Kansas players, and every portal decision.
  • Self-check: after applying, it re-reads every locked player and confirms nobody is still leaving and no graduate or Kansas player changed.

Result on your test save: 141 players locked at 57 teams (86 juniors, 55 sophomores; Texas 11, Notre Dame 9, LSU 7). 82 graduates still left. Checked in-game at Week 2: all 99 locked players not headed to the portal were still on their teams.

Next-season depth chart

lib/depth-chart.mjs · step 3

D1Who counts on next season's roster

The current roster, minus players who are graduating, leaving for the draft or entering the portal, plus committed transfers and signed recruits. Uncommitted transfers belong to no team.

D2Position rooms and starters

Players only count at their listed position. Rooms: EDGE = LE + RE, DT = DT + NT, LB = LOLB + MLB + ROLB, S = FS + SS, OT = LT + RT, OG = LG + RG. Every team starts QB 1, OT 2, OG 2, C 1, K 1, P 1; the rest depends on the scheme:

D3Roles inside a room

Each room is sorted by current OVR, then older first, then name. With S = the room's starter count:

RoleWho
StarterThe top S players
RotationThe next 1 player at HB, WR, TE, EDGE, DT, LB, CB, S; plus one swing lineman, the best non-starter across OT, OG and C
BackupEveryone else down to rank 2 × S (or S + rotation, whichever is larger)
BuriedEveryone below that

D4Path to start

A non-starter who isn't in his final year has a path to start when fewer than S players ahead of him will still be there the year after (players ahead of him who are seniors next season leave after it).

D5Starters lost and replaced

The tool also ranks this season's roster the same way. A lost starter is one of this season's starters who won't be back. Replaced means a transfer or recruit projects as a starter next season. Open = lost − replaced. That feeds the League Needs tab.

Ceiling (max overall)

lib/ratings-model.mjs · step 4

This follows the method in Maxi's Dynasty Progression Monitor, rewritten in our own code. Maxi's weights were extracted once into a data file (personal use only).

C1Overall and max overall formulas

Overall     = archetype intercept + Σ (rating × archetype weight)
              rounded (halves round down), kept inside the archetype's floor and ceiling

Skill group = 6 per archetype, in the same order as SkillGroupCap1–6
  group rating = weighted average of its ratings
                 primary ×15, secondary ×3 (+10 each), tertiary ×1 (+20 each)
  group level  = 1 + number of level thresholds the group rating reaches
  cap          = SkillGroupCapN (the most the group can ever reach)

Max overall = Overall with every group raised to its cap

C2Raising a group to its cap

the target is the threshold for the cap level. The tool adds +1 to the group's ratings in turn until the group rating reaches the target, starting from each rating in turn, and uses the midpoint of the results for each rating. Then it recomputes Overall.

Accuracy: the Overall formula reproduces 98.2–98.4% of the game's stored overalls exactly (the rest are off by 1). Examples: Kameryn Crawford 86 → 89 (matches Maxi's tooltip), Keisean Henderson 88 → 97, Matt Zollers 74 → 98, Julian Sayin 95 → 97.

Generous by design. Max overall assumes every upgrade gets bought. That's why it's only used for "could he ever crack their lineup" and the Star/Elite recruit check, never as a prediction.

Team side: would they want him?

lib/fit.mjs · step 4

For one player at one team: team score = role score × need multiplier, or 0 if he fails the level check (0 to 200). Position importance (T9) is applied on top only when comparing players at different positions.

T1His role there

The tool slots him into that team's next-season room using the same sort and cutoffs as section 5, without rebuilding the room.

Role thereRole scorePath-to-start bonus (by his year next season)
Starter100Freshman or Sophomore +30, Junior +15, Senior 0
Rotation60
Backup30
Buried0

Need in his room (AI teams only; Kansas's need is never used)

The room's need is the stronger of T2 drop-off and T3 weakness, or T4 stacked. Then, for each player, T7 upgrade and T8 succession can raise it further, and they override Stacked.

T2 Drop-off  = this season's starters vs next season's, slot by slot (best vs best, …)
            each slot: (this season OVR − next season OVR), kept between −10 and +10
            an empty slot next season counts as +10
            averaged over the slots, never below 0
            multiplier = 1 + drop-off / 10            (×1 … ×2 at a 10+ drop)

T3 Weakness  = expected level − average OVR of next season's starters
            expected level = league average starter in this room
                             + how far the team's other starters sit above/below their league averages
            fewer starters than slots counts as 10
            counts only at 5 or more:  multiplier = 1 + min(weakness, 10) / 10   (×1.5 … ×2)

T4 Stacked   = no starter lost, no newcomer starting, not weak,
            and the best backup is within 3 OVR of the weakest starter
            multiplier = ×0.25 (replaces the other two)

Room need = Stacked ? (stacked) : max(drop-off multiplier, weakness multiplier)

T7 Upgrade     raw upgrade = his OVR − the room's weakest starter (WR, DT, … use their lowest starter)
               level factor = 1 + 0.05 × (league average starter in this room − weakest starter)
                              kept between 0.25 and 1.5
               weighted upgrade = raw upgrade × level factor
               he'd start and the weighted upgrade is 4+:
               multiplier = 1 + 0.5 × min(weighted − 4, 6) / 6     (×1 at +4 … ×1.5 at +10)

T8 Succession  every starter is in his final year next season,
               and he's a freshman or sophomore next season who'd start or has a path to start
               multiplier = ×2

Need multiplier = max(room need, upgrade, succession); ×0.25 only if the room is Stacked
                  and neither upgrade nor succession applies

Labels you'll see: Drop-off Weak Steady (×1) Stacked Upgrade Succession. Rooms on Week 1: 1,368 drop-off, 332 steady, 167 stacked, 39 weak.

T7 examples: Ohio State's WRs are 99/87/87, so Wisloski (83) is measured against the 87, wouldn't start, and Ohio State scores 8. At Utah (WRs 78/71/69) he's +14 over the 69, which counts as +19.4 because that room sits below the league's 76.7 average. Hawkins (HB 93) at Boise State (starter 86, league HB average 79.9): +7 counts as only +4.9. At Air Force (starter 75): +18 counts as +22.4.

Why T7 and T8 exist: a team with every starter back can still want a better player (your Kansas QB situation). Before them, 1,402 cases on Week 1 where a portal player would start as a +10 upgrade in a "stacked" room scored only 25. Jide Abasiri (DT 88) at Duke, Louisville, Minnesota and Miami (OH) now scores 150 instead of 25. Stacked now only holds when he isn't a real upgrade (14,545 player-school pairs, down from 20,147). 51 of the 167 stacked rooms have only seniors starting next season; T8 covers those, e.g. Vernon Allen III (WR 78) at Ole Miss scores 120 as a backup with a path to start.

T10Starter level: real starter or stopgap?

At Week 1 every next-season room is emptied out by graduates and the portal, so weak players look like starters at schools that will refill the spot with better players. A projected starter only counts as one when his OVR is within 5 of the usual starter at his position for schools of the same prestige (the average of this season's starters at every school with that prestige). Below that he's a stopgap: both sides treat the job as a Backup (with a path to start if he isn't in his final year), he gets no upgrade boost (T7), and the team's ceiling check (T5) compares his max overall to that usual level.

Example: Christopher Clark-Jolivet (DT 70, max 77) "started" at BYU and USC because BYU lost Keanu Tuvasa (88) and Justin Kirkland (84) and only has a 65 freshman left. Prestige-7 schools usually start an 82 DT, so he's a stopgap there and his ceiling is too low: BYU and USC now score 0. His list is now rotation jobs at prestige 3–7 schools. League effect (Week 1): Top 10 average prestige by rating 85+ 6.8, 80–84 5.7, 75–79 4.3, under 75 3.4 (was 6.4 / 5.5 / 5.1 / 4.5); Utah's #1 picks 230 → 87.

T5Level check

If he wouldn't start there, the team only wants him when his max overall reaches their weakest projected starter minus 3. Otherwise the team score is 0 ("ceiling too low").

T6Tie-break: upgrade

upgrade = his OVR − the weakest starter he'd replace. Equal scores go to the bigger upgrade.

T9Position importance

Teams chase some positions harder: QB ×1.25, OT ×1.1, EDGE ×1.1, others ×1. Every team on one player's list gets the same weight, so it doesn't change the order of his Top 10; the ranking there uses the plain team score. It matters when a team has to choose between players at different positions (Signing Day placement and #1-pick balancing, steps 5 and 6).

P2Player score (0–100)

year weight w   = Freshman 0.20 · Sophomore 0.35 · Junior 0.50 · Senior (final year) 0.70
playing time    = role score there, max 100
prestige        = school prestige × 10, but only if he'd start, rotate or has a path to start; else 0
team strength   = average OVR of the school's projected starters, scaled 0–100 across the league
home            = 100 if the school is in his home state, else 0

rest            = 0.50 × dealbreaker value + 0.30 × prestige + 0.10 × team strength + 0.10 × home
player score    = w × playing time + (1 − w) × rest + jitter (fixed per player, school, season; within ±3)

How it was checked

  • Open and save: all test saves detected correctly; saving with no changes produced 0 differences in the player, team, recruit, Top 10 and leaving tables.
  • Draft lock: the saved file differed from its backup only in the 141 draft records, only in the two fields; verified in-game at Week 2.
  • Depth chart: every count matches the step 1–2 numbers on all six saves; projecting all 138 teams takes about 30 ms.
  • Team-side roles: the fast role calculation matches the depth chart for all 260,958 player-school pairs on Week 1, 0 mismatches.
  • Ceiling: 98.2–98.4% of stored overalls reproduced exactly; Crawford matches Maxi's tooltip.
  • Speed: the whole portal (1,891 players × 138 schools, both sides) scores in about 0.25 s.
  • Real save facts used for tuning: the game's own 90+ transfer commits all went to prestige 6+; 85–89 never below 4; uncommitted transfers at Signing Day mostly get deleted at rollover (1,042 of 1,310).

Not built yet, and known limits

  • #1 picks bunch up. Utah is #1 for 230 players and BYU for 176. Spreading #1 picks by open starter slots is part of the Week 1 generator (step 6), and Signing Day needs it too.
  • Roles use current OVR, not next season's progression.
  • Strict position rooms. A 3-4 team's extra DTs show as Buried; they don't count at EDGE.
  • Home pull is same-state only; only 1 transfer has Proximity to Home as his dealbreaker.
  • Position caps and the 85 limit aren't applied anywhere yet; they come in step 5 (Signing Day). Capacity check on the Signing Day copy: AI teams have only 194 spots under 85 as the save stands; after the planned recruit cleanup they have 1,656 for 1,310 uncommitted transfers, and with caps of 97 every position fits (WR is tight: 301 transfers for 303 spots).
  • Nothing is written to Top 10s yet. Everything in sections 5–9 is a read-only preview until step 6.
  • NIL expectation isn't modeled. The save has each player's NIL value (5–325) and each team's NIL spending and budget, but how the game uses them to block a CPU school isn't known yet.
  • Lists change during the portal. By Signing Day, 313 players' lists had gained 455 schools not on their Week 1 list, and 265 of the 581 Signing Day commits went to such a school. Every commit was to a school on his list at that time. So the Week 1 list is a starting point; the weekly refresh (step 7) and Signing Day (step 5) matter at least as much.
  • Open question for step 5: confirm on a copy that un-signing a recruit at Signing Day makes the game remove him at rollover.
