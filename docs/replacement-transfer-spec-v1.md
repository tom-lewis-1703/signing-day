Tom positively wants creative, deliberately simulated player and school individuality, including factors absent from save data. An occasional elite final-year player may knowingly accept limited football opportunity for a strong personal reason; this should be an uncommon, causally explained exception. ## 1. Chosen design and its tradeoffs

Simulate competing, binding offers in successive rounds. Below 85 players, a school can pursue different rooms concurrently, with one no-cut offer per room and enough reserved roster/board capacity for every offer to succeed. At 85 it can likewise pursue different rooms concurrently, with one contingent replacement offer per room, up to its remaining replacement allowance and free board slots. Players choose among their actual offers using their own utility and an outside option. School utility decides whom to pursue; player utility decides which offer to accept. Never average the two into a destination score. Section 7 defines reservation costs and ties completely.

After each round, accepted transactions change rosters and every affected valuation is recalculated. Commitments are irreversible within a plan. The process ends when no feasible mutually acceptable offer remains. There is no fixed round deadline, placement quota, talent redistribution target, or last-resort placement pass. Early commitments can foreclose better later combinations. The result is path dependent, not a globally optimal or stable matching under hypothetical cancellations.

Randomness represents preferences and school philosophies fixed throughout this market. Explicit social/dating and program-culture preferences give choices a reason beyond generic taste; rare strong lifestyle priorities can override ordinary football priorities. They are generated before offers, never invented to excuse an outcome. Opportunity and roster value respond to the evolving plan. No randomness changes saved player records or ratings, and no willingness is repeatedly rerolled until a player signs.

Reopen the complete transfer market except transfers committed to the selected user school. Virtually withdraw every CPU transfer commitment before calculating rosters, coverage, available board slots, or school demand. Former CPU signees compete on exactly the same terms as initially uncommitted transfers. Their native destination provides no preference, reservation, incumbent status, protected opportunity, fallback, or exemption. Preserve existing HS/JUCO commitments except surplus freshmen under §5.0 and the one contingent board release under §5.2. Trim remaining CPU overages through returning-veteran releases when coverage permits, and allow at most three further veteran replacements per school. Do not relocate released veterans, delete records, or clean up unsigned HS/JUCO players. Veteran releases do not create new transfer recruits.

The reported transfer pool is 1,310 initially uncommitted plus 581 initially committed, less transfers committed to the user's school. Confirm actual identities/counts. CPU withdrawals release their roster reservations and commitment-board slots. The previous 321-slot argument was based on the wrong scope and is withdrawn. With 138 boards of 35 and 3,928 HS/JUCO commitments, 902 total board places would remain for transfers across the league before accounting for the protected user commitments and excluding all remaining user-school capacity. Actual CPU availability is sum over CPU schools of (physical board capacity minus retained non-transfer commitments), subject to other constraints. Neither this conditional bound nor the game's old destinations is a target. Native CPU commitments are retained only as audit and write-reconciliation data.

An offer's modeled opportunity is a commitment the simulator must broadly honor. Later signings may cause small, explicitly bounded deterioration; they cannot cumulatively turn the offer into a different proposition. This protection begins when this algorithm signs someone. Former CPU signees have no role or roster protection from their native commitment. Preserved HS/JUCO commitments have protected roster places, not invented historical role promises.

This design does not depend on a real-world failure-to-land percentage, motive shares, causal football findings, private offers, or precise compensation. Consequently no new football-source investigation is needed to justify its constants. The report's percentages must not become calibration targets.

The model uses current ratings, class, development label, scheme, prestige, school grades, and coarse home-state affinity, together with explicitly generated preferences. It does not use skill ceilings as expected talent, starts/snaps, recruit gem/bust labels, real coach history, pipelines, geographic distance, hidden NIL fields, the old combined score, or the old baseline. No school social rating or player personality is inferred from those omissions. Established starters with identical observable inputs are not distinguished by last-season production; that limitation remains visible.

Only Signing Day is supported. This specification replaces the complete automatic Signing Day cleanup/placement/trim chain, not just its first placement loop. Other stages must not silently run the old algorithm through this path.

## 3. Input contract and ownership

### 3.1 Binding safety and identity

Require an explicit, valid selected user team before planning or editing. Only fixture tools may explicitly obtain the selection from `settings.json.testSavesTeam` and must validate it in the loaded save. No implicit production fallback to that setting.

Use the actual TeamIndex, never a roster-list row index. Use a player's full table/record reference as identity; names are display labels. Preserve same-reference identity across copies. Never allocate new Player records.

Exclude the user school from offers, roster trimming, withdrawals, replacements, and all automatic edits. Preserve its rostered players and all its existing commitments. A transfer committed to the user school is excluded from this market; a transfer merely listing that school or previously leaving it is not. A teamless candidate whose `PrevTeamIndex` is the user school may sign elsewhere regardless of an old CPU commitment. No user roster, board, or depth-chart membership may change.

Existing `keep` locks prohibit releasing a returning veteran or incoming recruit; they never preserve a CPU transfer's native commitment or exempt him from the market. A legacy keep lock on such a transfer has no allocation effect and is explicitly reported. Existing `notAt[player, school]` locks prohibit that pair, including the native CPU destination. Review markers do not affect the market. An obsolete lock referencing no present entity is reported and has no effect. Replanning with a lock preserves the seed and starts from the original snapshot followed by the same complete CPU-transfer withdrawal. Undoing a transfer destination adds `notAt` and reruns the market; it may leave him unplaced and never restores his game-selected school automatically. Undoing a veteran or recruit release adds `keep`. Individual CPU withdrawals cannot be disabled; canceling the entire unsaved plan preserves the original save.

### 3.2 Ownership and eligibility

Build a unique membership map by following every team's actual `CommittedPlayers` reference, separate from `RecruitingBoard` and its RecruitTarget references. Use it to identify original commitments even if Top Schools #1 differs; report the discrepancy. Preserve user-school commitments and HS/JUCO destinations according to this ownership. Every CPU transfer is then virtually removed from its original board and projected roster, regardless of native destination. Do not count that former commitment as an incumbent or a competing incoming player anywhere.

Duplicate board ownership, an active incoming prospect's committed status with no valid board owner, a non-committed player present on a commitment board, contradictory roster ownership, or malformed required ownership references blocks planning with the exact records identified. Do not silently invent a destination or cut someone to reconcile corrupt data. A Top Schools disagreement alone is not a blocker. Signed and HardCommitted both count as existing commitments. A rostered veteran absent from current commitment boards is not an active incoming prospect merely because he retains an old Signed recruit record. Teamless players classified by the reader as incoming HS/JUCO or Transfer prospects and all current board members are subject to the active-prospect ownership checks.

Validate ownership of writable storage as well as contents. Distinct schools must not alias one CommittedPlayers array, even if empty. Distinct prospect owners must not alias mutable TopSchoolsList/ProspectTargetSchool rows that a commit would change; validate the involved references against all loaded prospect owners, including existing commitments. Such aliasing blocks planning/preflight rather than permitting one edit to change another owner. Sharing a table is normal; sharing a mutable record across distinct owners is the error. These are targeted write-integrity constraints, not claims of observed corruption.

After withdrawing CPU transfer commitments, the initial CPU next-season roster contains returning rostered veterans plus preserved incoming HS/JUCO recruits, deduplicated. The user's existing roster/commitments remain outside the algorithm. Exclude graduating veterans, unresolved/confirmed early NFL departures using the reader's established `leavingForDraft` classification, and actual pending portal departures using its referenced leaving queue. Retain the distinction between historical recruit data and an active incoming prospect. Freeze coverage floors and make baseline trims only after this normalization.

The market pool is every active actual `Transfer_*` prospect not committed to the user school, including Signed/HardCommitted CPU prospects and initially uncommitted prospects. Valid transfer Recruit and TopSchoolsList references with at least one writable target-school slot, positive next-season eligibility, and a supported position are required to evaluate/sign a candidate. Former CPU commitments cannot be silently skipped and preserved through the old uncommitted-only filter. If a former CPU signee cannot be safely normalized or evaluated because of invalid fields/references/unsupported position, block planning and identify him. Initially uncommitted invalid/unsupported candidates may remain excluded and untouched with explicit reasons. Do not convert unsigned HS/JUCO, other teamless players, or veteran cuts into transfer candidates.

The supported Signing Day writer state for an active transfer is TeamIndex=255 with no roster/depth membership, including when Signed to a CPU school. If an active incoming transfer is already rostered, block planning with a precise unsupported-state report; do not silently omit him or call cutPlayer, which would overwrite his true PrevTeamIndex. A rostered veteran with only historical transfer recruit data is not an active transfer prospect. Supporting already-rostered incoming transfer withdrawal would require an explicit validated extension and is not inferred from direct-veteran-move evidence.

Valid critical fields are finite OVR in [0,99], a recognized SchoolYear, and RedshirtStatus in Current/Previous/Eligible/Ineligible. Invalid critical fields on a projected roster member block planning; invalid candidate fields exclude that candidate. Unknown development, school grades, or prestige use the explicit neutral defaults below and are counted in diagnostics. Unknown roster positions remain counted and uncuttable, with zero modeled contribution; unsupported candidates are excluded. Do not guess position conversions.

### 3.3 Class and two-season availability

Map Freshman/Sophomore/Junior/Senior to 0/1/2/3. Incoming HS/JUCO recruits keep their current `SchoolYear`. Returning veterans and all transfer candidates/commitments keep it when RedshirtStatus is Current, otherwise advance one. A non-incoming Senior whose RedshirtStatus is not Current has no remaining eligibility; he is not a projected returning veteran or eligible candidate. An existing committed transfer in that contradictory condition blocks planning rather than being silently discarded.

Let `y` be the resulting class index; modeled seasons remaining `E = 4 - y`. Only the first two matter. Do not add a speculative future redshirt year for Eligible players. Do not read Age or override class with a recruit-class suffix. These are forecast values only; no class/redshirt writes are part of this plan.

## 4. Common roster model

### 4.1 Rooms and scheme counts

Rooms: QB; HB; FB; WR; TE; OT={LT,RT}; OG={LG,RG}; C; EDGE={LE,RE}; DT={DT,NT}; LB={LOLB,MLB,ROLB}; CB; S={FS,SS}; K; P. Pool only within these rooms; do not write position changes or claim side-specific depth-chart assignments.

First-team slot count `S` has fixed QB=1, OT=2, OG=2, C=1, K=1, P=1. Other counts:

| Offense enum | HB | FB | WR | TE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OFF_SPREAD, OFF_POWER_SPREAD, OFF_SPREAD_OPTION, OFF_MULTIPLE_OFFENSE, OFF_PISTOL | 1 | 0 | 3 | 1 |
| OFF_AIR_RAID, OFF_VEER_AND_SHOOT, OFF_RUN_AND_SHOOT | 1 | 0 | 4 | 0 |
| OFF_PRO_STYLE | 1 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| OFF_OPTION | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| Unknown offense | 1 | 0 | 3 | 1 |

| Defense enum | EDGE | DT | LB | CB | S |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DEF_4_2_5, DEF_MULTIPLE_DEFENSE | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| DEF_BASE4_3, DEF_4_3_MULTIPLE | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| DEF_BASE3_4, DEF_3_4_MULTIPLE | 2 | 1 | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| DEF_3_3_5, DEF_3_3_5_TITE | 2 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| DEF_3_2_6 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| Unknown defense | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

Unknown schemes are explicitly flagged. These are roster valuation slots, not a claim about every formation the team will run. The counts deliberately resemble familiar room conventions; no existing settings weights are inherited implicitly.

For rooms with S>0, rotation count `R`: HB=2 (v1.4; was 1), WR=2, TE=1, EDGE=2, DT=2, LB=2, CB=2, S=1; all others zero. Backup count B=S and developmental-depth count D=S.

For TE with S=0, override the ordinary counts with two support slots: rank-value weights 0.30 and 0.12, opportunity indexes 55 and 20, then zero thereafter. For FB with S=0, use one support slot with weight 0.12 and opportunity 20, then zero. These are explicit allowances for supporting roles not represented by a coarse starting-formation enum, not fabricated first-team positions. A zero first-team count must not imply zero usefulness or automatic eligibility for every cut. These allowances do not guarantee that every school wants another TE/FB; positive net and player willingness still apply.

### 4.2 Ability forecasts

`q1 = min(99, OVR + 2)` for returning veterans and transfers if signed; `q1 = OVR` for incoming HS/JUCO. The same counterfactual signed forecast applies to every transfer offer. Do not rank an uncommitted transfer as permanently growth-ineligible when evaluating an offer that signs him.

For E>=2, `q2 = min(99, q1 + g)` where g=2 for Normal, 3 for College_Impact, 4 for College_Star, 5 for College_Elite. Unknown development uses 2 and is flagged. This is a modest second-season scenario, not measured growth by trait. E=1 players are absent from that scenario. No new future recruiting class, early departures, future transfers, scheme changes, or rating regressions are forecast. Skill caps are not predicted achievement and are unused. First-season forecasts deliberately use the same +2 for all veteran/transfer development traits.

### 4.3 School roster value

Sort each room by forecast q descending separately in each season; no seniority or name bonus. At equal q, identities break display order only and do not change value.

For S>0, rank weights h are 1 for the first S players, 0.30 for the next R, 0.12 for the next B, 0.03 for the next D, and 0 thereafter, except HB (v1.5), whose two rotation slots weigh 0.50 then 0.30. For S=0 TE/FB use the support overrides above. There is no reward for unlimited depth.

`V1(roster) = sum(h * max(0, q1 - 45))` over actual projected first-season players. `V2(roster) = sum(h * max(0, q2 - 65))` over actual players with E>=2, sorted independently. An absent player contributes zero. The second-season reference 65 represents a deliberately coarse allowance for future replacement opportunities that the model does not explicitly simulate. It is not an observed incoming average, a new player, a guarantee of future recruiting, or a save edit. It discounts treating today's empty future slot as a full unsupplied vacancy; it can also understate the value of a future weak starter. Keep that tradeoff visible.

Generate school development orientation `d = u("school-development", null, school)`. A higher d means more patience for future contribution and more aversion to displacing current veterans. Define `delta = 0.25 + 0.20*d` and `cutSurcharge = 2 + 2*d`. School value is `V = V1 + delta*V2`; the surcharge applies only to discretionary replacement offers. This shared philosophy affects recruiting decisions and player culture fit (§6), rather than being merely a label. All are simulated, not claims about actual coaches. No prestige multiplier, national talent rank, or old-school benchmark changes V. Stronger schools demand stronger players through their actual alternatives, not a mandatory prestige/OVR gate.

### 4.4 Player opportunity

At integer room rank j for S>0, opportunity is 100 in the first S slots, 55 in the next R, 20 in the next B, and 0 thereafter (including developmental-depth slots); HB's two rotation slots are 70 then 55 (v1.5). For S=0 TE/FB use §4.1's support indexes. At an exact rating tie, every tied player gets the arithmetic mean of the rank values occupied by the entire tie group. Identity never breaks a playing-opportunity tie.

To soften one-point rating cliffs, evaluate the focal player at q-3, q-2, q-1, q, q+1, q+2, q+3 against everyone else's fixed q and average the seven tie-aware results. Do not clip these seven comparison values; they are a rating-sensitivity calculation, not new saved OVR. Call the results O1 and O2 for the two seasons. Each evaluation includes the focal player exactly once in the hypothetical post-transaction roster. If E=1, O2 is absent.

This is an **opportunity index**, not a percentage chance to start, snap share, or promised depth-chart slot. Keep the ordinary q-based rank and names of competitors available for explanation. A pair of otherwise equal QBs has O1=60; one alone has 100.

Future feature `F = 0.70*O2 + 0.30*O1` for E>=2, otherwise zero. Blending discounts a succession story based only on today's roster. It is still optimistic about future recruiting and is labeled a scenario.

## 5. Roster capacity and releases

### 5.0 Surplus incoming freshmen (v1.6)

Only CPU committed, teamless incoming recruits with SchoolYear Freshman, observed OVR <=58 and known Normal development (Normal or College_Normal) qualify. Protect Keep selections, Impact/Star/Elite and unknown development, other classes, higher ratings, rostered players and all user-school players. This is not a blanket rating cutoff or a star-rating quota.

Freeze future coverage on each original post-withdrawal room at min(players with E>=2, S+1), or min(players with E>=2, support slots) for S=0. Before any veteran overage cuts, repeatedly remove an eligible recruit only if the room exceeds every modeled depth slot (h.length), both V1 and V2 losses round to zero, and the remaining future-eligible count meets that frozen floor. Choose lowest observed OVR, then lowest q2, then lowest player reference; recompute after each removal. This retains all modeled starter, rotation, backup and developmental slots, plus succession. Run the same cleanup after each school's accepted batch, before the next round of offers. Acceptance promises are recorded before this cleanup; removals cannot worsen them.

Each removal frees one roster and one commitment-board slot. There is no arbitrary per-school cleanup quota; no positive-value player is released by cleanup. Cleanup does not consume the one contingent recruit release or three veteran replacements. Keep cleanup rows distinct (kind recruit, reason surplus, round 0 or acceptance round), recording room count, depth floor, future count/floor and zero V1/V2 loss. Use the unchanged unsignRecruit writer. No records are deleted. If veteran overage trimming later fails, retain valid surplus cleanup but roll back that school's provisional veteran cuts.

### 5.1 Inherited overages

After CPU transfer withdrawals, freeze coverage per supported room at `min(initial room count, S+1)` for S>0; for S=0 TE use `min(initial room count,2)`, and for S=0 FB use `min(initial room count,1)`. This protects existing coverage without counting players now in the market. It does not invent missing players or force subsequent filling. Unknown-position roster members and keep-locked veterans are uncuttable. After §5.0 cleanup, retained HS/JUCO commitments are not candidates for baseline veteran trims. New algorithm signings become uncuttable when accepted.

On a virtual roster, while headcount>85, consider returning veterans whose removal preserves every coverage floor. Remove the veteran with smallest recomputed `V(before)-V(after)`; ties choose the lowest player reference. Recompute after every removal. No randomness or continuity surcharge applies to this unavoidable trim choice beyond the school's fixed delta.

If reaching 85 is impossible under these protections, discard **all** provisional veteran trims for that school (retain §5.0 cleanup), exclude it from this market, and report original headcount, excess, and binding protections. This includes >85 protected commitments. Do not partially trim it, relax protections, or call it fixed. Other schools may proceed. The excluded school remains the game's responsibility and a visible limitation.

Successful baseline trims are part of the eventual reviewed plan even if the school signs no transfer. They are separate from discretionary cuts and do not consume the three-replacement allowance. Their purpose is to make post-withdrawal inherited capacity concrete before evaluating offers; veteran cuts do not free commitment-board slots. Preserved HS/JUCO commitments stay intact. CPU transfer withdrawals are a distinct normalization operation, never a veteran cut and never charged against the three-replacement allowance. Even a school later excluded for an unresolvable overage loses its native transfer commitments to the market; rolling back that school's provisional veteran trims does not restore its transfer signees.

### 5.2 An offer transaction

An eligible school must have one available slot on its referenced `CommittedPlayers` array after all CPU transfer withdrawals. Read actual array capacity, count retained HS/JUCO entries, and reserve one for each algorithm commitment, including a transfer choosing his former CPU destination again. All CPU withdrawals happen before any new commits on apply. Veteran cuts do not create board slots.

**Recruit release (v1.4).** When a school has no free board slot, its only possible offers are contingent recruit releases: the transfer replaces one releasable recruit on the board and on the projected roster, so headcount is unchanged and no veteran is cut. A recruit is releasable when he is 1–2★ (`ProspectStarRating`), his development is known Normal (Normal or College_Normal), protecting Impact/Star/Elite and unknown labels, he has a modeled room and he is not keep-locked. Releases follow the cut-variant rules: an own-room release always keeps coverage, a cross-room release must preserve that room's coverage floor, the highest-net variant is chosen, and the quote includes the school's `cutSurcharge`. At most one contingent recruit release per school (`recruitReleaseLimit`), excluding surplus cleanup, separate from the three veteran replacements. The release happens only if the transfer accepts; the released recruit is un-signed with the proven `unsignRecruit` formula (Top10, zero offers, off the board), and the game deletes him at the Preseason rollover.

If roster headcount<85, evaluate no-cut additions; concurrent offers must reserve enough free board and roster slots under §7. Do not cut gratuitously. At exactly 85, evaluate one returning-veteran cut plus the addition. A school may issue several such offers in a round, one per room, up to its remaining replacement allowance and free board slots (§7). No two offers from one school in a round may touch the same room, counting both the incoming player's room and the released veteran's room; a variant whose release comes from an already touched room is unavailable for that offer. The cut must preserve coverage in the post-transaction roster and respect all locks. Only one veteran can be cut per offer, and at most three completed discretionary replacements are allowed per school. Baseline cuts and accepted incoming players cannot be cut again. No swaps between schools or multi-player packages.

Let n be the number of transfers already signed by this algorithm at the school. Let k be the number of earlier offers reserved by that school in the current round (zero for its first offer). For an individual proposed post-transaction roster R' against the round snapshot:

`gain = V(R') - V(R)`

`cost = 4 + 0.5*(n+k) + (cut ? cutSurcharge : 0)`

`net = gain - cost`

Only strictly positive net is worthwhile. Acquisition cost models limited appetite and the value of leaving room; the school-specific cut surcharge models disruption. These are utility units, not dollars. Quote and retain the cost when reserving an offer: a later offer is not retrospectively repriced if an earlier offer is rejected. This conservatively reserves acquisition appetite for every outstanding offer, while n increases only by actual acceptances. Store n, k, cost and gain separately in the trace. No round-based cost reduction or emergency exception exists. For each player-school pair choose the feasible cut variant with highest net, subject also to player and promise constraints. Ties favor no cut, then lowest cut-player reference.

The roster cut is contingent on acceptance. Losing offers cause no roster or save mutation. Baseline cuts are a separate prior plan decision. Final projected headcount for any participating school is at most 85; smaller rosters are allowed.

## 6. Player decisions and persistent uncertainty

### 6.1 Football features and deliberately simulated individuality

Features, all on a 0–100 index scale:

- T=O1; F as defined above.
- P=10*TeamPrestige for finite prestige in [0,10]; otherwise 50 and flagged.
- H=100 when valid player and school home-state codes match, 35 when both exist and differ, 50 when either is missing/invalid. Use `lib/data/team-states.json` for the school's state. Normalize both values by trim, lowercase, and deletion of whitespace, underscores, hyphens and periods; accept only normalized full names of the 50 US states and DistrictOfColumbia. Do not infer abbreviations (TX), numeric codes, International, or other unknown values. No invented distance, region, or state-size correction. This exact rule keeps unavailable geography neutral rather than accidentally treating it as known distance.
- M: game-motivation feature. PlayingTime maps to T; ProximityToHome maps to H. PlayingStyle uses the grade for the player's archetype; ProPotential uses its position group; other recognized school-grade motivation keys use the corresponding school grade. Unknown/unset/NIL/unusable categories score 50 and are labeled unavailable. Do not synthesize NIL offers or treat a missing label as an observed NIL motive.

Grade order is F,Dminus,D,Dplus,Cminus,C,Cplus,Bminus,B,Bplus,Aminus,A,Aplus, mapped to `100*index/12` without intermediate rounding. Missing/unknown grades score 50. ProPotential groups: QB→QB, HB/FB→RB, WR→WR, TE→TE, OT/OG/C→OL, EDGE/DT→DL, LB→LB, CB/S→DB, K→K, P→P. Supported general motivation keys are exactly the keys of the reader's MySchoolTracking grade map; do not use IdealRecruitingPitch as an additional factor.

Raw weights in order T,F,P,H,M:

| E | Raw weights |
|---|---|
| 1 | 5,0,2,1,2 |
| 2 | 4,1,2,1,2 |
| 3 or 4 | 3,2,2,1,2 |

Multiply each nonzero raw weight independently by `0.75 + 0.50*u("weight-"+feature, player, null)`, then normalize all five to sum to one. Freeze them for the entire plan. Repeated PlayingTime/Proximity in M intentionally amplifies that game cue. Missing M remains neutral with its weight intact; do not redistribute it to prestige or opportunity. These are simulated priorities, not discovered personality traits.

Call that five-feature weighted sum `football`. Add two explicitly simulated environment preferences. These require no new save fields or external data:

- School `socialScene = 100*u("school-social", null, school)` describes a quieter/smaller-circle environment at 0 through a lively/expansive social environment at 100. Player `socialIdeal = 100*u("social-ideal", player, null)` describes his preferred environment, not an amount of virtue or football ambition.
- `datingFit = 100*u("dating-fit", player, school)` is that player's subjective attraction to the campus dating environment. It is not a factual rating of students' attractiveness and is not a shared ranking all players believe. Define lifestyle fit `L = 0.60*(100-abs(socialScene-socialIdeal)) + 0.40*datingFit`.
- Player `cultureIdeal = 100*u("culture-ideal", player, null)` describes preference for an immediate-results environment at 0 through a patient development environment at 100. Program culture fit `C = 100-abs(100*d-cultureIdeal)` uses the same school philosophy d that affects future value and displacement cost. This is appeal of a simulated operating environment, not a guarantee of playing time or training growth. Actual opportunity remains in football.

Generate all traits before evaluating offers. Let `aOrd = 0.04 + 0.06*u("social-weight", player, null)`, `b = 0.04 + 0.04*u("culture-weight", player, null)`. Independently classify `rareLifestyle = u("social-priority", player, null) < 0.01`. If false, a=aOrd; if true, `a = 0.60 + 0.10*u("social-weight", player, null)`. The same social-weight draw supplies intensity within the two branches. The one-percent prevalence and all magnitudes are ex-ante design choices, not measured motive shares. A rare player's nonfootball priorities can genuinely outweigh an immediate starting opportunity; ordinary players retain 82–92% football weight. No trait/class is assigned in response to OVR, eligibility, a disappointing placement, or a need to explain it. These traits remain fixed for every school and round within the plan.

`U = (1-a-b)*football + a*L + b*C + taste - returnFriction`

`taste = 2*(2*u("player-fit", player, school)-1)` is a small residual preference for otherwise close choices; it has no invented backstory.

`returnFriction = 8` when school is valid PrevTeamIndex, otherwise 0.

`outside = 52 + 8*(2*u("outside", player, null)-1)`

Do not clip U to [0,100]. A player accepts only U>outside, using the numeric comparison rule below. Equality means prefer remaining unplaced. There are no hard motive/prestige gates, no relaxed low-OVR dealbreakers, and no end-of-market lowering of outside. Generated environments, preferences, culture and dating fit must be labeled **simulated** in explanations; never attribute them to real people or campuses as observed facts. No actual NIL offer or relationship is inferred. The ordinary GUI may say that a player prioritized his simulated social/dating fit or program culture, but only when the numeric trace supports that explanation.

For a rareLifestyle player, recompute a counterfactual U at each actual offered school with a replaced by aOrd, retaining b, football, L, C, taste, outside, return friction and the actual offered roster. Apply the same willingness and offer-choice ties. Report separately (a) acceptance enabled by the exceptional weight, and (b) choice among actual offers changed by it, including choosing unplaced when every counterfactual offer fails. If neither changes, do not credit an unusual destination to the exceptional motive. For ordinary players, show the actual factor contributions without inventing an exceptional story. This is an explanation of a single decision, not a re-run of the league with the trait removed.

Report all E=1 signers with current OVR>=85 and final O1<35, including raw competitors, original/final opportunity, actual alternatives, and the causal counterfactual where relevant. Rare trait prevalence is not a guarantee that such outcomes are rare. For the rarity review count, a rareLifestyle player qualifies if the aOrd counterfactual rejects his accepted offer OR changes his chosen actual offer, including choosing unplaced; count that union once per player and retain the component counts separately. Show counterfactual alternatives' O1 as well: changing destination does not itself prove lifestyle caused the later low opportunity. Two or more qualifying elite/final-year/low-final-opportunity players in any sampled seed trigger design-owner rarity review before acceptance. This is a subjective review trigger, not an allocation quota or automatic rejection. An isolated case still needs a credible school-side reason and a genuine motive effect. Inspect cases without this motive on their own merits, rather than adding a motive afterward.

Returning to his actual former school in PrevTeamIndex is legal if both sides agree despite the friction. His native CPU commitment destination is not his former school for this calculation. Choosing that native destination again is an ordinary new allocation, with normal acquisition cost, capacity use, and promise protection from this acceptance onward. There is no return exception, continuity bonus, or rescue pass. Existing in-game Top Schools, influences, native commit stage, and CPU board ownership are not preference or ranking inputs. The commit writer puts the algorithm's accepted school on the list. Only the user-school transfer commitments are exempt from reconsideration.

### 6.2 Promise constraints

When a transfer accepts, store his O1, F, U, outside, contract type, and the actual full post-round roster containing every accepted offer at his school and no rejected ones. Cross-room offers do not change his own-room features; nonetheless the stored snapshot must be the real accepted batch.

For E>=2 with acceptance O1<35, explicitly label the offer **developmental reserve**: it does not guarantee retention of an immediate backup slot. Every other signer has an immediate-opportunity contract. For every later transaction require for each previous algorithm signer:

- immediate-opportunity contract: new O1 >= original acceptance O1 - 12;
- E>=2 under either contract: new F >= original acceptance F - 12;
- every signer: new U > his original outside.

Use the standard numeric comparison rule also for the O1<35 boundary. The contract type is frozen at acceptance. This permits an explicitly developmental reserve to tolerate loss of a small immediate role when his future scenario and willingness remain acceptable; it does not waive future protection or force a player to accept changed terms. An E=1 player, including a rare lifestyle case, retains the immediate-opportunity guard.

Compare to the original acceptance snapshot, never the preceding round. Non-opportunity features are fixed, and all relevant U values use the same traits/weights. Cuts cannot worsen opportunity indexes, but check each whole transaction/batch. No promises apply retroactively to native CPU commitments, and no guarantee concerns actual game training, depth charts, or next year's recruiting.

## 7. The market loop

1. Validate ownership and user exemptions, virtually withdraw every CPU transfer commitment, and normalize the full remaining pool. Derive the seed/freeze traits. Prepare post-withdrawal coverage/trims and exclude unavailable schools/user school. n=0 and discretionary replacement count=0 for each participating school. Native CPU signings do not increment n or occupy slots.
2. Begin a round from one immutable virtual-state snapshot. Evaluate each candidate-school transaction with §5.2's structural rules, both parties willing, and all earlier promises honored. Keep the k=0 standalone evaluations for diagnostics. A structurally legal pair with either side unwilling is not offerable.
3. For a school below 85, set `cap = min(free commitment-board slots, 85-headcount)` and k=0. Until k reaches cap: consider no-cut candidates in rooms for which this school has not yet reserved an offer; recompute their quoted net at cost `4+0.5*(n+k)` against the unchanged snapshot; discard nonpositive-net or otherwise unacceptable pairs; select greatest `priority = quotedNet + 1.5*(2*u("school-fit", player, school)-1)`. Ties favor greater quotedNet, then lowest raw player reference. Reserve that offer, its room, one board slot and one roster slot, and increment k. If no candidate remains, stop selecting for this school. Selection is deterministic greedy reservation, not portfolio optimization. A candidate belongs to exactly one supported room, so the identity tie also determines room order.
A school with no free board slot instead uses step 4's selection with `cap = recruitReleaseLimit - completed contingent recruit releases` and recruit-release variants only (§5.2); with no releasable recruit or no remaining release it makes no offer.

4. For a school at exactly 85 with board capacity and remaining replacement allowance, set `cap = min(free commitment-board slots, 3 - completed replacements)` and select cut-plus-add offers by the same greedy reservation as step 3: one per room, quoted cost `4+0.5*(n+k)+cutSurcharge`, the same priority and ties. Each offer uses §5.2's highest-net cut variant among those whose release room no earlier offer of this school in this round has touched; once selected, its incoming and release rooms are both touched. Because no room is touched twice, each offer's gain, player utility, coverage and promise effects are independent of which other offers are accepted. An excluded overage school makes none.
5. Issue every selected offer simultaneously. Each offered player accepts greatest U; ties favor greater O1, then smaller TeamIndex. Every issued offer exceeds outside, so he accepts exactly one. He does not compare to a school that did not offer or wait for a theoretical future offer. Schools neither average in his utility nor know other schools' selected offers. The acceptability screen remains simulator knowledge, not a claim about coaches knowing private motives.
6. Apply the accepted batch simultaneously. All offers below 85 were no-cut, in distinct rooms, with capacity reserved for all to succeed. At 85 each offer carried one release, and no room was touched by two offers. Roster value is additive by room, and personal/school traits are fixed, so other accepted rooms do not change a player's own-room utility or promises. Verify the actual complete accepted batch against capacity/coverage/promises before accepting the round. Increase n by actual acceptances, and replacement count by actual accepted cuts. Preserve every quoted n/k/cost; rejected offers do not get charged and do not cause retroactive repricing of accepted offers. Store original promise snapshots from the actual full accepted roster and all actual offered alternatives. Remove every accepted player from the remaining candidate pool before the next round. No backfill after rejection within this round; reevaluate next round.
7. Repeat until no offer exists. Every nonempty round signs at least one new candidate; hence at most the initial candidate count of nonempty rounds. This is a termination bound, not a deadline. No candidate with an actual offer stays uncommitted to consume unchanged offers repeatedly.

Recalculate after each round, not after an arbitrary school iteration within it. Caching must preserve snapshot semantics and invalidate affected roster values/opportunities/pair feasibility. Display queries and tracing must not change results. There is no final reshuffle, post-hoc promise relaxation, cleanup outside §5.0, alternate forced placement, or optimization of already accepted commitments.

This removes the one-position-at-a-time bottleneck when capacity permits, including for full schools, not every timing effect. One candidate per room, one release per room, shared physical capacity, quote escalation, and binding signings still restrict options. For each signer report `missedPreferenceGap = max(0, bestStandaloneU - acceptedU)`: bestStandaloneU is the greatest U across each school's selected highest-net mutually acceptable k=0 variant under §5.2 at the beginning of that signing round, using the immutable state and same promises/locks. Do not substitute a U-maximizing lower-net cut that the school would not select. For tied best alternatives use the normal player-choice ties. Record that school, its transaction, and why it did not issue an offer (preferred same-room target, another offer's release came from his room, used capacity for other rooms, or higher reservation quote made the transaction unacceptable). Do not relabel a hypothetical willing school as an actual offer.

A signer with gap>10 is a **real miss** when that better school, in any later round including the final round in which no offers remain, would have offered him had he still been available. Evaluate this at that round's snapshot with the school's ordinary selection rule: his standalone k=0 transaction there must be mutually acceptable, and the school's greedy selection with him added must pick him. A better school that filled its capacity with players it ranked higher is not a real miss; the gap shows only that he would have accepted it. Record the round in which the school would first have offered. Report both the willing-better-school count (gap>10) and the real-miss count. More than 10% of signers being real misses in a seed triggers design-owner competition-policy review before acceptance. If there are no signers, report zero denominator and an unavailable share. These ex-ante review thresholds are not a player-optimality guarantee or automatic reason to change destinations.

Also identify signings where `delta*(V2(after)-V2(before)) > 0.5*gain`, using the quoted individual transaction. For each later round evaluate same-room hypothetical upgrades at standalone k=0 with that round's actual n and cut surcharge: require positive V1 gain, structural feasibility, player willingness, and positive net while ignoring only earlier promise constraints. Mark the candidate-school upgrade promise-blocked only if at least one such variant exists and none of its positive-V1, otherwise acceptable variants satisfies every promise. A blocked cut variant is not enough if another permitted cut allows the upgrade. For the trace choose highest net among the otherwise acceptable positive-V1 variants, then the usual cut tie; record all its blocking signers/contracts and failed promises, and flag when a blocker was a future-heavy signing. Store the proposed cut when applicable. No issued offer or quoted reservation is fabricated. This diagnostic is not limited to developmental reserves and does not claim reduced future weights eliminated every succession tradeoff.

For every remaining candidate, the final report states structural exclusions and counts of schools failing capacity/locks/coverage, school value, player outside option, and prior promises. These constraints can overlap: preserve the multi-reason diagnostics, not a misleading single causal partition. Evaluate a consistent best-net structural variant for veto diagnostics when the acceptable set is empty, and label it diagnostic, not an offer. Record the best achievable U and net as separate values and identify their distinct schools. Zero viable pairs at termination must be verified by independent final enumeration of the defined feasible transactions. It is not a claim that cancellations could not improve the allocation.

For all unplaced players with current OVR>=85, plus the ten highest-current-OVR remaining unplaced players (deduplicated; OVR ties by reference), also enumerate **board-only vetoes**. Remove only the free-board-slot test, hold the final roster and all other rules fixed, and identify schools/transactions that would otherwise be feasible and mutually acceptable. Do not actually issue or apply these counterfactual offers. This isolates the commitment-capacity policy's effect from lack of interest. A school excluded because it could not trim to 85 remains excluded in this diagnostic.

## 9. Limits and reopening decisions

This is an ex-ante design with concrete first parameters. No empirical claim that those parameters already produce an enjoyable market is made. Acceptance must inspect both outcomes and fidelity, including unpopular results. Calibration is permitted only in response to a specific documented concern; Claude must not silently tune policy.

Known limits: one edited dynasty is the main evidence lineage; rating/eligibility forecasts are rough; no production-history, actual NIL offers or real coach relationship model; state-only geography; room pooling can hide left/right imbalance; scheme/support slots are approximations. The q2 reference level and school horizon discount do not simulate a future class, and player O2 remains optimistic about future recruiting. Concurrent offers reduce but do not remove timing effects; greedy capacity reservation can miss better portfolios. Preserved HS/JUCO can force veteran losses or leave a school over capacity. Keeping all of them is a revisable policy, not a conclusion established by evidence against blanket cleanup. Binding promises may still block upgrades. Generated social/culture traits are plausible simulation choices, not empirically established psychology, and the rare branch's outcome frequency is untested. All unplaced/cut future fates remain under game control. CPU withdrawal remains unvalidated, already-rostered incoming transfers block planning, and at most three discretionary replacements remain permitted even if further positive-value deals exist.

The fixed outside option prevents universal placement from becoming a covert objective. No artificial calendar deadline ends the market, but offer reservations can still limit which choices reach players; report that distinction. These are chosen tradeoffs; an alternative is not automatically better because it places more players or improves average roster ratings.

No new experiment is commissioned in this design phase. Acceptance prescribes an early withdrawal proof, compact rule checks, then first-plan and bounded multi-seed outcome review, followed by integrated save/UI/game proof after implementation authorization. The specification can be implemented without an additional broad research assignment, but it is not accepted on the strength of this document. Reopen a concrete mechanism or premise when evidence or Tom's experience warrants it. Retain the design owner's final acceptance responsibility.
